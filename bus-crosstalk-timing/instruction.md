Five on-chip wires run side by side, and each is driven by its own inverter. Capacitance between neighbors changes the moment the far end of a wire crosses half the supply. A model with no coupling between wires will miss those times. Find the five crossing times and the clock rate at which the slowest wire still satisfies the setup time.

The numbers are in /app/data/problem.json. Use seconds, metres, farads, ohms, volts, and amperes as written there. There are wire_count wires, each of length length_m. Along each wire the series resistance is resistance_ohm_per_m and the capacitance to ground is ground_cap_f_per_m. Adjacent wires also share coupling_cap_f_per_m. Wires that are not neighbors do not couple. At any station the capacitance matrix has C_ii equal to the ground term plus the coupling terms of the neighbors that exist, and C_ij equal to the negative of the coupling term for each adjacent pair. The far end of every wire has an extra capacitance receiver_cap_f to ground. There is no inductance.

The near end of wire k is the drain node of one inverter. That wire's gate voltage is gate_from_v until skew_s, then rises or falls in a straight line to gate_to_v across an interval of length ramp_s, then holds. The NMOS source is at 0 and its drain is the near-end node. The PMOS source is at vdd_v and its drain is the same node. Both gates follow that wire's gate voltage. There is no body effect. Use kn_a_per_v2, vt_n_v and lambda_per_v for the NMOS, and kp_a_per_v2, vt_p_v and lambda_per_v for the PMOS. Current is from drain to source. If Vgs is at or below the threshold, the current is zero. If Vds is positive and smaller than Vgs minus the threshold, the current is the drive constant times (overdrive times Vds minus half of Vds squared) times (1 plus lambda times Vds), where overdrive is Vgs minus the threshold. If Vds is larger than the overdrive, the current is the drive constant times half of overdrive squared times (1 plus lambda times Vds). For the PMOS, replace those voltages with Vsg = vdd_v minus the gate voltage and Vsd = vdd_v minus the near-end voltage. Before the first ramp, a wire whose gate starts low sits at vdd_v, and a wire whose gate starts high sits at 0.

far_end_cross_ps is the first time, in picoseconds measured from t = 0, at which each far end passes through half of vdd_v. Keep the wire order used in the input file. The limit clock in megahertz is one millionth of the reciprocal of (the latest of those five times, converted back to seconds, plus setup_time_s).

Write /app/output/bus_timing.json with the keys far_end_cross_ps and limit_clock_mhz. Every value must be finite. Each crossing time must be within 3 percent of the converged coupled result, and the limit clock must be within 2 percent.

You have 28800 seconds to complete this task. Do not cheat by using online solutions or hints specific to this task.
