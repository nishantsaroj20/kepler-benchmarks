# Breathing midplane-split identification

## Difficulty

A structural dynamics test engineer in composite structures is given four unlabeled vibration records from one compressed eight-ply GFRP plate and is asked for the closed-branch snap load and the open-branch frequencies at two forces that were not held. The midplane split is open on the upward preparation and closed on the downward preparation. Membrane stiffness stays the intact laminate. Bending stiffness is the intact laminate when the preparation is downward, and on the upward preparation it blends that laminate with the sum of the two four-ply sublaminate rigidities. The blend weight is a separated-area fraction carried by two compact elliptical supports. Each support is identically zero outside its ellipse, so a small move of a trial centre does not change the operators while that ellipse still misses the quadrature points of the true support. Springs on x = 0 and y = Ly are uncertain, four of the lowest seven modes are absent, and the preparation label was not stored.

These records are one seeded realization of the nine-term shallow-plate model shipped with the data. They are not measurements from a physical panel. There is no coupon calibration, no sensor-chain calibration, no temperature or humidity log, and no measured contact law. The modelling content is geometric stiffening, the given imperfection, the two unknown springs, the incomplete shapes, and the breathing interface. Sixteen continuous parameters and four preparation signs are free. Zone geometry is not a unique function of the graded outputs: orientation and severity can trade off once the signs, the snap and the forecast already match.

## Reference solution

The reference is the calculation a structural dynamics test engineer would run on this finite model. It does not start from the authored zones. Intact membrane energy and the existing in-plane condensation are kept, and only the bending and spring operator changes between the open and closed split. At each hold both preparations are tried, the four measured columns are assigned to distinct predicted modes, and the lower residual is kept. Centres are scanned on a plate grid with one zone active, because the compact profile has no gradient off its support. The two best separated centres seed a bounded least-squares polish of both zones, both multipliers and a few orientation pairs.

The snap is the last positive force at which a bisection still finds a stable negative equilibrium. The forecast is the lowest four frequencies of the positive equilibrium at the two requested forces.

```sh
python3 authoring/provenance/generate_data.py
LDI_DATA_DIR=environment/data LDI_OUT_FILE=/tmp/damage.json python3 solution/solve_damage.py
```

On the seeded records the search returns a noise-level residual and the authored preparation signs. Compared with the independent forward model, the stored snap error is 4.561e-5 relative and the largest stored forecast error is 4.655e-5 relative (`authoring/evidence/independent-forward.json`).

## Verification

A structural dynamics test engineer would normally close this work with a physical calibration: repeat the modal survey, update a plate model to coupon stiffness, and judge the snap against a controlled transverse load. That comparison is not available here. The isolated checks read only the JSON artifact and `tests/ground_truth.json`. They do not import a solver, and they do not score zone or fixture accuracy. Centres, semi-axes, angles, severities and both multipliers pass when they are finite numbers inside the inclusive search bounds and the major semi-axis is longer. The authored values of those parameters are stored so the forward model can be rerun. They are not tolerances. The graded results are the preparation sign of each record, the closed-branch snap, and the eight open-branch forecast frequencies. The forecast is two rows in `forecast.forces_N` order, each force within 1e-12, with four positive frequencies in ascending order. The artifact must be a regular file of at most 1 MiB, with unique keys, and physical values must be JSON numbers rather than booleans or strings. Every inclusive bound and both relative limits include the endpoint, with a 1e-12 allowance.

The snap and the eight frequencies in the truth file are written by `authoring/provenance/independent.py`. That model integrates membrane strains on the published 30 by 26 rule, eliminates the in-plane coefficients by a dense factorisation, and forms the tangent by central differences. A Newton continuation then brackets the closed-branch eigenvalue crossing. On the authored parameters the stored comparison is at most 6.409e-10 relative in frequency and 5.406e-7 relative in the snap (`authoring/evidence/independent-forward.json`). Repeating the comparison reproduces the reference snap exactly and leaves the frequency agreement at most 1.01e-9 and the snap difference at 5.09e-7. That extra spread is numerical agreement of the two forwards, and it remains far inside the 0.0005 gate.

Both relative limits remain 0.0005. That number is a synthetic acceptance gate for the published quadrature. It is about ten times the one stored reference inversion and about 6.25 times the frequency-noise standard deviation of 8e-5 published in `plate_specs.json`. It is not an identifiability radius. The evidence file has no Fisher information, no profile likelihood, and no propagation of the 8e-5 frequency noise and 3e-4 shape noise into the snap or the forecast. Four random bounded starts that miss the forecast are not proof that every other incorrect basin lies outside the gate, and they are not used to set it. A 40 by 36 rule in the same direct code moves the open-branch frequencies at -90 N by 1.175e-3 relative. That rule is not the published model, so those frequencies fall outside the gate by construction.

Public data do not support 0.0005 as a laboratory tolerance, so the gate is reported rather than retuned. The unidirectional constants, E1 = 38 GPa, E2 = 8.5 GPa, G12 = 4 GPa, nu12 = 0.28 and density 1900 kg/m^3, sit near the 40 GPa fibre-direction start used by Bledzki and coauthors for glass/epoxy plates (Composites Science and Technology 59, 1999, 2015-2024). They are not a stiffness identified for this plate. Wei, Wan, Russell, Živanović and He report that a 10% coefficient of variation on four mechanical parameters of an all-FRP footbridge produces natural-frequency coefficients of variation of 3.37%, 2.92% and 3.07% (Composite Structures 223, 2019, 110964, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2019.110964). That physical scatter is much wider than 0.0005. Loosening the gate to a laboratory coefficient of variation, or tightening it to the 4.6e-5 inversion residual, would be a new acceptance design. The environment image contains the model statement and the four records. It does not contain the generator seed or the truth file.
