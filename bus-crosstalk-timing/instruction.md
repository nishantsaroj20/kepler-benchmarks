The aim is to examine the transient signal propagation along five parallel on-chip interconnect lines, each controlled by a separate CMOS inverter, and calculate the switching delays and the maximum working frequency. Arrival times can be affected by strong dynamic crosstalk between adjacent interconnects. Models that do not include inter-wire capacitive coupling will result in inaccurate crossing times. Compute the first mid-supply crossing time for the far end of each wire, find the greatest clock frequency that satisfies the receiver setup requirement of the slowest line, and save the result to `/app/output/bus_timing.json`.

The file `/app/data/problem.json` contains all the geometric dimensions, electrical characteristics, transistor coefficients and operational timings. The interconnect bus consists of wire_count parallel wires, each length_m metres long. Lines contain distributed series resistance resistance_ohm_per_m and capacitance to ground ground_cap_f_per_m. Distributed mutual capacitance coupling_cap_f_per_m accounts for crosstalk between physically neighboring wires only; non-adjacent wires have no direct coupling. At any point along the line, the self capacitance C_ii is equal to the ground capacitance plus the sum of mutual capacitances to the immediate neighbors that exist, and the mutual term C_ij is equal to the negative of the shared coupling capacitance between neighboring pairs. Line inductance is ignored along the bus. The far end of each wire is terminated in a lumped load capacitance to ground of receiver_cap_f.

Each line is driven at its near end by a dedicated static CMOS inverter whose drain node is connected directly to the line input. The input gate voltage is gate_from_v for wire k until time skew_s, then changes in a straight line to gate_to_v over a transition interval ramp_s, and then stays at that final level. The inverter has an NMOS device with its source grounded at 0 V and a PMOS device with its source connected to vdd_v. The two drains meet at the near-end node, and both gates follow that input waveform. Body effect is ignored. The NMOS uses drive constant kn_a_per_v2, threshold voltage vt_n_v, and channel-length modulation parameter lambda_per_v. The PMOS uses drive constant kp_a_per_v2, threshold voltage vt_p_v, and the same channel-length modulation parameter lambda_per_v. Drain-to-source current is zero when the gate-to-source voltage Vgs is less than or equal to the threshold. In the linear region, where the drain-to-source voltage Vds is positive and less than the overdrive (Vgs minus the threshold), the current equals the drive constant times (overdrive times Vds minus half of Vds squared) times (1 plus lambda_per_v times Vds). In saturation, where Vds is greater than the overdrive, the current equals the drive constant times half of the overdrive squared, times (1 plus lambda_per_v times Vds). The PMOS uses the source-referenced voltages: Vsg is vdd_v minus the gate voltage, and Vsd is vdd_v minus the near-end node voltage. Before the ramps begin, each line is at rest: at vdd_v if its initial gate voltage is low, or at 0 V if its initial gate voltage is high.

Evaluate far_end_cross_ps as an ordered list of the first time, in picoseconds from t = 0, at which the far-end voltage of each line crosses half of vdd_v. Keep the same wire order as the input. limit_clock_mhz is the clock frequency in megahertz given by one millionth of the reciprocal of the sum of setup_time_s and the latest of the five far-end crossing times after that latest time is converted back to seconds.

Write `/app/output/bus_timing.json` with the two keys far_end_cross_ps and limit_clock_mhz. Every value must be finite. Each crossing time must be within 3 percent, and limit_clock_mhz must be within 2 percent.
