Your goal is to evaluate the steady-state thermal and mechanical field of an internally cooled carbide turning tool in cross-section, determining its temperature distribution, thermal expansion at the cutting corner and the maximum cutting speed before reaching a thermal limit and write the predictions to `/app/output/tool_result.json`. Because the cooling hole significantly redirects heat flow and the thermal conductivity of the carbide degrades noticeably as temperature rises, simplified models that ignore the internal channel or assume constant room temperature thermal properties will fail to reach the required accuracy. The choice of numerical discretization, mesh design and nonlinear solver is entirely up to you.

All geometric dimensions, material properties, operational settings and boundary conditions are specified in `/app/data/problem.json`. Units across the dataset are consistent: lengths are given in metres and temperatures in degrees Celsius. The tool cross-section spans the rectangular domain from x = 0 to width_m and y = 0 to height_m. The top edge at y = height_m forms the tool rake with process heat entering exclusively over the initial contact segment 0 <= x <= heat_patch_length_m. A circular internal coolant channel is positioned at (hole_center_x_m, hole_center_y_m) with a radius of hole_radius_m. The rear vertical boundary at x = width_m represents the tool shank face and is held at a fixed temperature of shank_temperature_c.

The thermal response is modeled under steady two-dimensional conditions. The thermal conductivity of the carbide varies with temperature, defined as conductivity_ref_w_mk divided by the quantity one plus conductivity_softening_per_k multiplied by the temperature rise above conductivity_ref_temperature_c, where that temperature rise must not be allowed to become negative. Inward heat flux along the heated rake contact patch, in watts per square metre, equals heat_flux_per_speed_w_m2_per_m_s multiplied by the cutting speed in metres per second. Convection occurs along the internal hole perimeter to fluid at coolant_temperature_c with heat transfer coefficient coolant_h_w_m2k. All remaining exposed surfaces—including the vertical flank face at x = 0, the unheated rake segment past the heat patch, and the bottom face—convect to surrounding air at air_temperature_c with heat transfer coefficient air_h_w_m2k.

The resulting steady temperature field drives thermal deformation under linear elastic plane-strain conditions. The carbide is isotropic with elastic parameters young_modulus_pa, poisson_ratio, and linear thermal expansion coefficient alpha_per_c, with the material being completely stress-free at stress_free_temperature_c. The effective plane-strain thermal expansion strain equals alpha_per_c multiplied by the local temperature rise relative to stress_free_temperature_c and multiplied by the factor (1 + poisson_ratio). The rear shank face at x = width_m is fully clamped, enforcing zero horizontal and vertical displacements. The parameter edge_growth_um represents the displacement of the cutting corner at (x = 0, y = height_m) in the negative-x direction, reported in micrometres, evaluated at reference_speed_m_s.

You will evaluate two specific operating regimes. First, at reference_speed_m_s, determine the temperature profile along the heated rake and the resulting edge growth. Second, determine the limit cutting speed where the cutting corner at (x = 0, y = height_m) reaches edge_temperature_limit_c. This threshold speed falls strictly within the interval bounded by speed_bracket_low_m_s and speed_bracket_high_m_s.

Write your final output to `/app/output/tool_result.json` as a valid JSON file containing three top level keys: rake_temperature_c, edge_growth_um and limit_speed_m_s. The key rake_temperature_c must contain a list of five finite temperatures in degrees Celsius evaluated along the heated rake surface at 0.15, 0.35, 0.55, 0.75, and 0.95 of heat_patch_length_m. The key edge_growth_um must report the corner displacement in micrometres as a finite positive number, and limit_speed_m_s must report the limiting cutting speed in metres per second as a finite positive number. Your solution must satisfy these accuracy criteria: each rake temperature must match within 3 percent, the edge growth within 3 percent, and the limit speed within 2 percent of the converged benchmark solution.
