# Slurry rate forecast

## Difficulty

A reaction engineer who sets the next charges on a stirred slurry hydrogenation autoclave does this work. The campaign is synthetic. It is built like a pilot-plant export from a 1.5 L toluene hydrogenation of 4-nitrotoluene: relative hydrogen-uptake noise of 0.011, a thermowell that is not the reference probe, a chromatograph factor that changes on the service date, a spreadsheet rate column left on the old factor, a blank tachometer row, a dissolution row taken with the baffles out, and a CO-uptake assay that falls as conditioning speed rises. Those are realistic defects for this equipment, not a clean textbook table. The hard part is a chain of modeling choices the folder never names. Hydrogen at the catalyst surface follows a saturating rate law, the surface coefficient and the adsorption coefficient each carry their own temperature dependence, and the surface concentration sits behind the measured gas-liquid and liquid-solid films. Activity moves with CO uptake, including the fresh-catalyst charges, which the historical runs never used. The operator's weekly note calls the job first order and kinetic. That reading does not survive the pressure series once the films and the instrument records are taken seriously. Dropping any one of those links, or swapping in a first-order, power-law, or temperature-independent adsorption form, leaves at least one charge outside the stated tolerances.

## Reference solution

Reconcile each historical uptake with the chromatograph factor in force on that date, map TIC-201 through the calibration table onto the reference probe, and form the hydrogen partial pressure by subtracting the toluene vapor pressure from the absolute headspace pressure. Convert that partial pressure with the solubility table. Use the baffled-vessel gas-liquid and liquid-solid coefficients, and scale the catalytic step by CO uptake relative to the fresh catalyst. The corrected surface concentrations identify a Langmuir-Hinshelwood hydrogen rate whose saturated surface coefficient and adsorption coefficient each follow a two-parameter temperature law. Solving that rate forward, with the same two films, gives the hydrogen consumption and the three concentration-drop shares on every requested charge. The reference route is output-error nonlinear least squares with a closed-form quadratic for the implicit rate. The independent route estimates the same four parameters from per-temperature Hanes lines and resolves the implicit rate by Newton iteration.

## Verification

The ground truth is the latent two-film forward model in authoring/provenance/generate_campaign.py, seeded with numpy Generator seed 20261111 and relative rate noise 0.011. It is not a fit produced by the reference solution. tests/truth/expected.json stores that forward model on the eight charges. A rate is within tolerance when the absolute error is at most the larger of 0.040 times the latent rate and 0.0006 mol m^{-3} s^{-1}. A fraction is within tolerance when the absolute error is at most 0.030. The fractions must also sum to 1 within 0.0001. These bands were set from both correct routes on the shipped files. In authoring/evidence/calibration_results.json the output-error route has maximum relative rate error 0.0248964391865222 and maximum absolute fraction error 0.017743795342130775, and it misses no charge. The Hanes-Newton route has 0.02476621604846857 and 0.01765098477577237, and it misses no charge. The largest relative rate gap between the two routes is 0.004048961917707893, and the largest absolute fraction gap is 0.0023320378227265626. The relative tolerance 0.040 lies above the larger truth error and above that cross-route gap. The fraction tolerance 0.030 lies above both fraction errors and above that cross-route gap. The absolute floor 0.0006 lies above the K1 absolute errors 9.608109412197544e-06 and 9.22336261777873e-06. It binds only for K1, where 0.040 times the latent rate 0.009640259654698127 is 0.0003856103861879251. Every baseline in authoring/evidence/baseline_failures.json fails all eight charges: constant 0, constant 1, the mean of the latent values, the median of the latent values, a constant equal to each latent rate copied into every numeric field, and each numeric input column copied into every numeric field.
