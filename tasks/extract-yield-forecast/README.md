# extract-yield-forecast

## Difficulty

A separations engineer who sets the next mixer-settler charges does this work. The campaign is synthetic. K-17 is a monoprotic carboxylic acid extracted from aqueous broth into 4-methyl-2-pentanone. The records include a thermocouple polynomial, two pH windows, a spreadsheet pH column that used the wrong slope sign before 2024-05-19, an organic assay blank, a recovery column built from that blanked assay, aborted rows, unmixed rows, and a separate equilibrium shake study. The campaign note states the plant approach law and the chemist's conclusion that the neutral acid dimerizes in the ketone. It does not write the closed-contact balance. The charges sit mostly where recovery is still moving, and several of them leave the pilot window in controller temperature, solvent ratio, stirrer speed, and contact time. Deriving that balance, chaining the equal solvent split, and identifying the constants from the shakes and the agitated contacts is the work. A monomer-only distribution fit to the reconciled aqueous residuals misses 37 of the 52 charges. Fitting the spreadsheet pH, the controller temperature, and the blanked recovery column misses 46. Copying the mean recovery of the agitated pilot rows misses 49. The forecast is the predictions file itself.

## Reference solution

solution/solve.sh executes solution/forecast.py. That program drops aborted and unmixed pilot rows, keeps the equilibrium shakes, maps tic_c through the thermocouple polynomial, and maps ph_mv through the pH window that contains the row date. The measured removal is the aqueous residual over the feed. Those removals identify pKa, the monomer partition at 298.15 K, its temperature slope, the dimerization constant, and kLa_ref together, by nonlinear least squares. Each stage solves the closed balance in which the organic loading is the neutral monomer plus twice the dimer. A two-stage charge splits the solvent into equal portions and contacts the raffinate with the fresh second portion. The reported organic concentration is the removed solute divided by the combined solvent volume, with no assay blank.

## Verification

The ground truth is the forward model in authoring/provenance/generate_campaign.py, evaluated at the seeded constants in authoring/provenance/seeds.json on the 52 charge inputs. solution/forecast.py is not imported to build that truth. tests/truth/expected.json stores the forward model. A recovery is within tolerance when the absolute error is at most the larger of 0.02 times the true recovery and 0.012. An organic concentration is within tolerance when the absolute error is at most the larger of 0.02 times the true concentration and 0.60 mol m^{-3}. The bands were set from two structurally different correct routes, recorded in authoring/evidence/calibration_results.json. The reference joint least squares has maximum relative recovery error 0.0030347837687722994 and maximum relative organic error 0.003035231390944039, and it misses no charge. The other route fits the equilibrium on the shake study with the rate law turned off, then fits only kLa_ref on the agitated pilot rows. That route has maximum relative recovery error 0.005556995228951198 and maximum relative organic error 0.005557028972866781, and it misses no charge. The largest absolute gap between the two forecasts is 0.00118840947523835 in recovery and 0.19424654080047787 mol m^{-3} in organic concentration, and the largest relative recovery gap is 0.0036531446922929037. The relative tolerance 0.02 is more than three times 0.005557028972866781. The aqueous assay noise is 0.004 relative. At the smallest true recovery, 0.1086193713043323, three standard deviations of that assay is 0.010696567544348012. The absolute recovery floor 0.012 lies above both 0.010696567544348012 and the recovery gap 0.00118840947523835. The absolute organic floor 0.60 lies above three times the organic gap 0.19424654080047787. A monomer-only fit on the reconciled residuals misses 37 charges. The spreadsheet-column fit misses 46. The mean recovery misses 49. tests/test_outputs.py reads /app/output/predictions.json and does not execute submitted code. A missing file, a value that is not finite, a duplicate key, and a symlink all fail the comparison.
