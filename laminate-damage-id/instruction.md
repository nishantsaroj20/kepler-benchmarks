Your goal is to identify two unordered compact elliptical zones of a breathing midplane split and two boundary mounting multipliers for a single symmetric eight-ply laminated GFRP plate, determine the static preparation branch sign for each of four modal observation records, predict the critical positive snap-through stability-loss force on the negative branch, and forecast the lowest four natural frequencies of the positive preparation at two forces that were not held during the test. How you formulate the nonlinear equilibrium path, solve the modal inverse problem, and compute the limit point is entirely up to you.

All model specifications, load values, search bounds, and baseline properties are provided under `/app/data/`. The file `/app/data/plate_specs.json` defines laminate material constants, geometry, stacking sequence, initial compressive stress resultants, known imperfection amplitudes, transverse hold forces, the two forecast forces, record identifiers, parameter intervals, and synthetic noise scales. Full documentation of the variational potential, the breathing split, the function spaces, the boundary springs, and the observation conventions is in `/app/data/README.md`.

The structural model is a geometrically nonlinear von Kármán shallow plate expanding transverse displacement about a known stress-free initial imperfection, subjected to prescribed compressive initial-stress resultants and uniform transverse pressure $ F/(L_x L_y) $ for the specified total force $ F $. The finite forward model retains transverse translational inertia alone and expands incremental out-of-plane displacement in nine double-sine modes while relaxing nine functions for each in-plane displacement component quasistatically. The laminate is an eight-ply symmetric layup with a single split between ply 4 and ply 5, counted from the bottom. Each damage zone is one compact field parameterized by centre coordinates $ (x_c, y_c) $, elliptical-support semi-axes $ a $ and $ b $ ($ a > b $), orientation angle $ \theta $ in degrees measured counterclockwise from $ +x $ (periodic modulo 180°), and one separated-area severity $ d $. The profile is $ g=(\max(0,1-(u/a)^2-(v/b)^2))^2 $ and is identically zero outside that ellipse. There is no geometric clipping beyond that support and no non-overlap rule inside it. The separated fraction is $ s=1-\prod_j(1-d_j g_j) $. It is not a ply-group knockdown and it does not scale the membrane stiffness. On a preparation with $ q_{11}>0 $ the split is open and the bending stiffness is $ D=D_{\mathrm{intact}}+s(D_{\mathrm{split}}-D_{\mathrm{intact}}) $. On a preparation with $ q_{11}<0 $ the split is closed and $ D=D_{\mathrm{intact}} $. Membrane $ A $ is the intact laminate on both preparations. $ D_{\mathrm{split}} $ is the sum of the bending stiffnesses of the lower four plies and the upper four plies, each taken about its own centroid. Density and Poisson ratios do not change. Edge rotational restraints on $ x=0 $ and $ y=L_y $ are scaled by shared multipliers `x0_multiplier` and `yL_multiplier` across all holds; the other two edges stay nominal.

Synthetic observations consist of seven out-of-plane displacement sensor channels recording four distinct modes chosen from the lowest seven tangent eigenmodes at each held transverse load. Static displacement fields are not measured. Resonance identifiers associate frequencies with shape columns within that record only. Mode ordering, missing modes, column scale, column sign, and the physical preparation branch sign are unrecorded. At zero transverse force, the positive and negative preparations are the minimum-potential states in the half-spaces $ q_{11}>0 $ and $ q_{11}<0 $. Each record is prepared by quasistatically tracing one of these stable states to the specified hold force without a stability loss. The branch sign is the sign of the first sine coefficient $ q_{11} $ of that held equilibrium. To forecast `snap_load_N`, start on the negative preparation at zero load ($ q_{11}<0 $, split closed) and monotonically increase the uniform positive transverse force until the tangent stiffness first loses positive definiteness on that same metastable branch. That force is the limit-point snap load, not the flat bifurcation, the Maxwell equal-energy load, or a dynamic jump force. Separately, `open_branch_forecast_Hz` must contain the lowest four ascending natural frequencies of the positive preparation at each force listed under `forecast` in the specification. Those two forces are not among the held records.
